STRONG PASSWORDS

A strong password is the simplest thing you can do to protect an account, yet it is the step most people rush. A weak password is not just a short one - it is any password that is easy to guess, easy to look up, or reused somewhere else. Attackers do not usually sit and type guesses by hand; they run lists of millions of common passwords against your account in seconds, so "easy to guess" includes anything a computer could find in a dictionary or a list of previous data breaches.

WHAT MAKES A PASSWORD STRONG

Length matters more than complexity for tricks like swapping a letter for a number. A long passphrase made of several unrelated words is harder to crack than a short word with a "!" and a "1" added to the end, because every extra character multiplies the number of guesses an attacker has to try. Strong passwords also avoid anything tied to you personally - birthdays, pet names, or your school - since those are the first things an attacker will try.

COMMON MISTAKES

The two mistakes we see most often are reusing one password across several sites and writing passwords in a place that is not actually private, such as a sticky note or an unlocked notes app. Reuse is dangerous because if one site is breached, every other account using that same password is now at risk too - this is exactly the problem the next lesson, on password managers, is built to solve.

QUICK CHECKLIST

  • Use at least 12 characters, longer where the site allows it.
  • Mix in a passphrase rather than a single dictionary word.
  • Never reuse a password across more than one account.
  • Avoid personal information an attacker could look up.
